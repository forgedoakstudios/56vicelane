Leonida's soundscape — ocean, city streets, swampland — is going to be one of GTA6's biggest technical showcases, and for The Last Drive you need to actually hear your convoy over all of it. Here's a section-by-section breakdown of the four headsets worth considering, plus a full comparison at the bottom.

🎧
Headset · PlayStation
Sony Pulse 3D Wireless
★★★★☆ 4.5

Sony built this specifically around PS5's 3D Audio engine, and it shows — no lag, no interference, and a wireless USB dongle that pairs cleanly without any Bluetooth handshake nonsense. Dual noise-cancelling mics keep Last Drive comms clear even with the mic boom in a normal talking position.

Why it matters for GTA6: PS5's 3D Audio is one of the more genuinely useful audio features this generation — footsteps, gunfire direction, and traffic all become directional in a way stereo can't match. This is the headset it was tuned for.
Pros
  • Built for PS5 3D Audio specifically
  • Zero-latency wireless via USB dongle
  • Comfortable for long sessions
Cons
  • PlayStation-only — no real multi-platform use
  • No active noise cancellation

Headset · Multi-Platform
SteelSeries Arctis Nova Pro Wireless
★★★★★ 4.7

The premium pick, and the only one here that genuinely moves between systems without a re-pair. Active noise cancellation, 360° Spatial Audio, and simultaneous multi-device connect mean you can be on PS5 for GTA6 and still hear your phone's Last Drive Discord call at the same time.

Why it matters for GTA6: If you split time between console and PC, or want your phone's Discord audio mixed in during a Last Drive session without swapping headsets, this is the only option here that actually does it cleanly.
Pros
  • True multi-platform, simultaneous device connect
  • Active noise cancellation
  • 360° Spatial Audio on top of platform-native audio
Cons
  • The most expensive headset on this list by a wide margin
  • Overkill if you only play on one system

Headset · Xbox
Xbox Wireless Headset
★★★★☆ 4.4

Microsoft's official headset connects directly to Xbox with zero dongles or Bluetooth pairing steps — it just works the moment you turn it on. Spatial audio and auto-mute (it mutes your mic automatically when you're not talking) round out a headset built entirely around removing friction.

Why it matters for GTA6: The most reliable "no setup" option for Xbox players — good enough audio quality without ever touching a settings menu, which matters when you just want to load in on launch night.
Pros
  • Direct Xbox connection, no dongle or app required
  • Auto-mute mic
  • Reliable, simple setup
Cons
  • Xbox-only
  • No active noise cancellation

Headset · Budget Pick
HyperX Cloud Alpha
★★★★★ 4.7

Dual-chamber 50mm drivers, an aluminum frame, and memory foam ear cushions built to survive an all-night session. It's currently one of the best-selling budget gaming headsets on the market, and it earns that with sound quality that outperforms its price bracket rather than just being "good enough."

Why it matters for GTA6: You don't need platform-specific audio tech to enjoy Leonida's soundscape — you need clear, well-balanced sound that doesn't fatigue your ears over a long session, and this delivers that without the premium price tag.
Pros
  • Excellent sound quality for the price
  • Durable aluminum frame
  • Comfortable for long sessions
Cons
  • Wired, not wireless
  • No platform-specific audio tech (3D Audio, Spatial Audio)

The Bottom Line

If you're PlayStation-only, the Pulse 3D is the tuned-for-purpose pick. If you regularly split time between systems — or want your phone's Discord mixed in during a Last Drive session — the Arctis Nova Pro is worth the premium. Xbox players who just want it to work should grab the official Wireless Headset. And if budget is the deciding factor, the HyperX Cloud Alpha punches well above its price and is the pick we'd recommend to most people starting from scratch.
