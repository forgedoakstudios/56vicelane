Take-Two: GTA6 Could Bring In $3.5 Billion At Launch

📅 November 10, 2026 ✍️ Marcus Webb ⏱️ 5 min read

Ten days before launch, Take-Two just told Wall Street what it actually expects GTA6 to do — and the numbers are enormous, even by this game's own absurd standards.

💰 The Headline Number

According to PC Gamer, Take-Two told investors on its Q2 fiscal 2027 earnings call (held after market close on November 9) that it expects GTA6 to generate as much as $3.5 billion during the launch period. That's not a lifetime sales projection or a multi-year franchise estimate — that's the company's own guidance for the window immediately around launch, the kind of number that would make GTA6 one of the single biggest entertainment releases in history regardless of medium.

Separately, according to Eurogamer's coverage of the same call, CEO Strauss Zelnick said pre-orders are tracking "well above expectations" and that the game is set to "set a new baseline" for Take-Two going forward. That second line is worth sitting with — Zelnick isn't just saying GTA6 will do well, he's framing it as resetting what "success" looks like for the entire company afterward.

📈 Why This Call Matters More Than the Last One

Back in August, on the Q1 call, management called preorder activity "exceptional" but stopped short of raising guidance — citing cancellable orders, possible demand pull-forward, and the simple fact that no units had shipped yet. Ten days from launch, with actual pre-order volume now largely locked in, that caution has apparently given way to much more direct, bullish language. The gap between "exceptional, but we're not changing guidance yet" in August and "$3.5 billion" in November is the story here.

🧮 What We Could And Couldn't Verify

We weren't able to pull the original PC Gamer and Eurogamer articles directly to quote the full context verbatim, so we're treating the $3.5 billion figure and the "tracking well above expectations" / "new baseline" language as reported by those outlets rather than independently confirmed word-for-word. The underlying event checks out, though: Take-Two was scheduled to report Q2 FY2027 results after close on November 9 — exactly ten days ahead of the November 19 launch — and that timing lines up with prior reporting on the company's earnings calendar. The prior quarter's call (August) is also on record describing pre-order demand as "exceptional," so today's apparently more confident language is consistent with the trend, not a sudden reversal.

📝 Editor's Note

A company projecting its own revenue on an earnings call is a different kind of "confirmed" than a leak or a direct product announcement — it's forward guidance, not a locked-in result, and actual launch-period revenue won't be known until Take-Two reports it after the fact. Worth remembering before treating $3.5 billion as a done deal.

The Bottom Line

Ten days out, Take-Two's own language is about as confident as it gets short of an actual number in hand — up to $3.5 billion projected for the launch period, pre-orders reportedly running ahead of plan, and a CEO openly framing this as a reset point for the whole company.
