From The Editor's Desk: 6 Days Out

📅 November 13, 2026 ⏱️ 7 min read
Danny Marchetti
Editor-in-Chief

Six days. I want to start with the most normal story I've read all week, because I genuinely don't trust myself to recognize "normal" anymore and I need the practice. Eurogamer ran a piece this morning on a UK broadband operator — Openreach, the infrastructure arm behind most of the country's internet — publicly saying GTA6 launch traffic has "been on our radar for a long time." That's it. That's the whole story. A telecoms company is planning ahead for a predictable demand spike. I read it twice looking for the part where it turns out to be fake, and there isn't one. It's just infrastructure people doing infrastructure planning. I don't know what to do with a story that doesn't have a catch in it anymore, but I'm choosing to take it as a good sign.

📡 If The Broadband Companies Are Bracing, So Are We

We already ran the piece on why the disc doesn't make sense against GTA6's actual download size — the physical media is basically a key, not the game. Openreach's comment is the other half of that same story: this isn't a few hundred thousand people downloading a patch, it's a meaningful fraction of an entire country's internet users hitting "install" within the same 48-hour window. If a national broadband operator is flagging it by name in the press six days out, that's not hype, that's someone's actual capacity-planning spreadsheet. I have a new rule for myself: if the thing worrying me is also worrying the people who run physical internet infrastructure, it's probably a real thing to worry about, not a me thing.

Smaller, and I'll admit this one's just fun: the Gay Tony cameo theory Trevor and I have both been circling hasn't been confirmed or debunked by anyone with actual authority to do either. It's just sitting there, unresolved, next to an unconfirmable gameplay clip that a major outlet couldn't verify yesterday. I used to be annoyed by loose threads this close to launch. Six days out, I've started finding them almost comforting — at least a cameo theory is low-stakes uncertainty. Not everything this week gets to be that.

💊 What We're Doing About It

Practically: we're not waiting for launch night to find out if our own site can handle a traffic spike. We're already load-aware on the pipeline side, the Last Drive signups are holding up fine under current volume, and if a national broadband provider is planning around this, we're planning around it too — same instinct, much smaller scale. Drawer four is now at five antacids, which I'm aware is an unhinged number to be tracking in a public article, and I'm doing it anyway because at least the count is honest.

— Danny, who would like it on record that "the broadband company is prepared" is the single calmest sentence he's gotten to write about GTA6 in six days, and he intends to enjoy it before something less calm inevitably shows up tomorrow.

The bottom line

Six days out, and for once the scariest story of the day is a boring one — a broadband company got ahead of a traffic spike it saw coming. That's what preparedness looks like when nobody's panicking. The Last Drive is still the night before, November 18th, still free, still no killing, still just the road. What's the last "mundane but telling" launch-week story you've seen — the one that isn't flashy, but makes the countdown feel real anyway?
