Trevor's Take: 8 Days And The Internet Is Straining

📅 November 11, 2026 ⏱️ 7 min read
Trevor
Unsolicited, As Always

Eight days. Okay. Okay. I need everyone to sit with me on this one because I've been up since 4 AM thinking about it and I think I finally have it figured out and I need to say it exactly once, calmly, before Danny tells me I can't put it in the column. Dexerto reported that the GTA6 preload is straining actual global internet infrastructure right now. Today. As I am writing this. The INTERNET. The whole one. Not a server, not a region, the broad general concept of "the internet" is apparently experiencing load because of a file transfer. Does nobody else find that insane? Am I the only one who finds that insane? I called my cousin who works in IT and asked him if this was normal and he said "yeah, big game, big file, happens sometimes" and I said "that's exactly what someone covering for it would say" and he hung up on me, which, notable, I wrote it down.

Here's my actual theory, and I want the notebook to reflect that I arrived at this one sober and well-rested, which for me lately is its own kind of achievement: you don't accidentally strain the global internet. You either strain it by accident, which would be embarrassing and they'd apologize, or you strain it on purpose, as a test, to see how much load the pipes can take right before you need them to carry something much bigger on the 19th. I am not saying what that "something bigger" is. I don't know what it is. I have a page for guesses and the page currently has eleven guesses on it and none of them are good and I'm not reading any of them out loud in a public column, Danny already took away my whiteboard privileges once this month.

📓 New Notebook Section, I Had To Add One

I started a new section specifically for "things that are definitely just coincidences" and the preload-strains-the-internet thing is entry number one, right above the $3.5 billion thing and the AI-crackdown thing, which — and I want to be fair to myself here — I noticed FIRST, before any of this, which either means I'm good at my job or means I'm the reason this is happening, and I've genuinely stopped being able to tell which one is true and that is maybe the most honest sentence I've written in this column all week.

Also: Danny's on drawer four now. I counted. I didn't say anything to him directly because I think he's trying to keep it quiet, but I want it on the record that I noticed, and that if the operation wraps on the 19th and somebody debriefs me, "Danny's drawer count" is going in my report as a leading indicator, because frankly it's the most reliable instrument we've got in this building.

🫡 A Message, Still Just In Case, Getting More Specific

If this is it — if the 19th is when the operation wraps and I don't get a say in how it ends — I want it noted that I knew it was coming before anyone else did, that I tried to warn people in the most responsible way available to me, which was a satire column, and that The Last Drive, November 18th, the night before, is still where I'll be. Not as a bit this time. I mean it. Come find me there. Bring the car. We're doing this properly, operation or no operation.

Anyway. Eight days, a strained internet, drawer four, eleven unread guesses on a page I'm not showing anyone. I'm fine. I'm completely fine. I just think somebody should be writing this down in real time, and apparently that somebody is me, unsolicited, as always, see you tomorrow at seven, assuming the internet holds.

— Trevor. (My cousin still hasn't called back. Writing that down too.)
