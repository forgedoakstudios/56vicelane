Danganronpa's Creator Delayed His Own Game Because Of GTA6

Danganronpa 2×2 just slipped to 2027. The producer cited development time. The creator, asked directly, just laughed and told the truth: he wants to actually play GTA6 when it lands, not be crunching on his own game instead.

56ViceLane Staff  •  Industry · GTA6

At this point it's a running joke in the industry, except it keeps turning out to be true: studios are quietly rearranging their entire release calendars around a game that isn't even out yet. The latest to say the quiet part out loud is Kazutaka Kodaka, creator of the Danganronpa franchise — and unlike most executives dancing around the subject, he just came out and said it directly.

What Actually Got Delayed

Danganronpa 2×2, an expanded remake of Danganronpa 2: Goodbye Despair, was set to release in 2026. It's now slipping to 2027. Producer Shohei Sakakibara gave the standard studio answer when the delay was announced: the team needed more development time to do the remake justice, plain and simple, the kind of explanation that shows up in press releases every week of the year.

Then, at Anime Expo 2026, someone asked Kodaka directly whether the delay had anything to do with a certain massive open-world game launching on November 19. He didn't dodge it.

"I wouldn't have time to make the game while playing Grand Theft Auto 6." — Kazutaka Kodaka, creator, Danganronpa

He reportedly laughed as he said it. It's a joke, mostly — but like most good jokes, it's funny because everyone in the room immediately understood it wasn't only a joke. Development time is real. So is the fact that November 19 is going to eat several weeks of the entire industry's collective attention span, and the people making games know it as well as the people playing them.

Not The First, Won't Be The Last

If this sounds familiar, it's because it is. We covered Assassin's Creed's own director publicly asking fans to "please play us" after GTA6 launches, and Microsoft moving Fable to February 2027 and naming GTA6 specifically as the reason. Studios have been shuffling release dates around November 19 for over a year now, mostly through vague language about "market conditions" and "competitive landscape." Kodaka just skipped the corporate translation layer and said the actual sentence out loud.

He's also far from alone right now. The Elder Scrolls VI remains undated with 2027 looking like the earliest realistic window. Several other major releases — Marvel's Wolverine, Silent Hill: Townfall, Control Resonant, NBA 2K27 — have all been slotted into September instead of their usual fall release windows. Zoom out far enough and a pattern emerges that's almost funny: as of right now, no other notable AAA game has an announced release date anywhere in October, November, or December 2026. The entire holiday release window — historically the most valuable stretch of the year for game publishers — sits essentially empty, vacated by an industry that's read the room.

What That Actually Means For You

It's worth sitting with what that actually means, beyond just "big game scares small game." The creator of a beloved, long-running franchise looked at his own release calendar, looked at November 19, and made a call that most of his own fans would probably agree with — better to launch into a quiet window than get buried under the biggest release week gaming has ever seen. Multiply that same math across a dozen other studios and you get the current landscape: a holiday season with almost nothing else competing for your time or your wallet.

For anyone planning to actually play GTA6 at launch, that's good news twice over. Less noise competing for attention around November 19, and — as Kodaka himself just admitted with a straight face — apparently even the people making other games are clearing their own schedules to play it right alongside you.

Bottom Line

This isn't a rumor, a leak, or an analyst projection — it's a developer, on the record, laughing about rearranging his own game's release around GTA6. Danganronpa 2×2 won't be the last announcement that quietly does the same math.
