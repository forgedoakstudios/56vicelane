While We Wait: Fans Are Porting GTA5 To Switch And Building Inter-Game Portals

📅 August 2, 2026 ✍️ 56ViceLane News Desk ⏱️ 5 min read

Thirteen years is a long time to sit on your hands, and this community has never really known how to just wait quietly. Two separate fan projects surfaced this week that prove it — one group is working on an unofficial GTA5 port to Nintendo Switch, and a modder built something even stranger: a way to travel instantly between three classic GTA games as if they were one connected world.

🎮 The Unofficial GTA5 Switch Port

Per The Gamer's reporting, a group of fans is actively working on getting GTA5 running on Switch hardware that was never officially supported for it. This is a genuinely difficult technical undertaking — Switch's hardware is a significant step down from what GTA5 was built to run on, even accounting for the game's age, which means this is real reverse-engineering and optimization work, not a simple port job.

⚠️ Worth Being Clear About

This is an unofficial, fan-made project with no involvement from Rockstar, Take-Two, or Nintendo — and projects like this have historically run into legal takedowns once they get enough attention. We're covering it as a snapshot of community dedication, not endorsing piracy or unauthorized use of Rockstar's assets. Support it or don't, but know what it actually is.

🌀 The Modder Who Built Inter-GTA Portals

The second project, covered by PC Gamer, is a mod that lets players travel instantly between Rockstar's first three GTA games — effectively stitching them into one connected "multiverse" you can move through without reloading separately. The modder's own framing of it, paraphrased in the coverage, was blunt: "we got inter-GTA portals before GTA6." It's a joke, but it's also a pretty clever demonstration of just how much untapped potential is sitting in games Rockstar built decades ago.

🕰️ What This Actually Says About The Wait

Neither of these projects exists because the community thinks GTA6 won't be good — they exist because thirteen years without a new mainline entry left a lot of restless energy with nowhere else to go. Fans didn't just wait. They kept building things out of what they already had, whether that meant squeezing a decade-old open world onto hardware it was never meant for, or literally connecting three separate games together because nobody told them they couldn't.

The bottom line

Neither project changes anything about GTA6 itself, and both are worth watching more as cultural artifacts of this specific, unprecedented wait than as things to actually go download. If you're building something yourself while you count down to November 19, we want to hear about it.
