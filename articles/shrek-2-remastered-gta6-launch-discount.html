Shrek 2 Remastered Timed Its Launch Around GTA6

📅 November 12, 2026 ✍️ Marcus Webb ⏱️ 4 min read

Most games scheduled anywhere near GTA6 have spent the past year quietly moving out of the way. Shrek 2 Remastered did the opposite — it launched nine days before the biggest release of the decade, with a trailer that parodies GTA6's own marketing beat for beat.

🎬 The Trailer That Started It

Aspyr's remaster of Shrek 2 released November 10 on PS5, Xbox, Switch 2, and PC, and its reveal trailer leans directly into GTA6 comparisons — TweakTown described it outright as "PlayStation's newest GTA6-style trailer," matching the slow-pan, neon-lit, needle-drop format Rockstar made famous with its own reveals. It's not subtle, and it's not meant to be — the whole point is riding the wave of attention GTA6 has generated, rather than competing with it directly.

💰 The Price, If You're Curious

According to GameSpot, the base price is $29.99, with a 10% launch discount bringing it to $26.99 in the US (PS5 owners need an active PlayStation Plus subscription for that rate). GameSpot also flags a steeper third-party deal — $22.79 at Fanatical, well under the official price — for anyone who just wants the nostalgia hit without paying full price.

🎮 What This Means For You

Nothing here actually competes with GTA6 for your November 19 — it's just a genuinely funny bit of industry timing. If you grew up on Shrek 2 and want something to play in the week leading up to launch, this is clearly built to scratch that itch on purpose.

🐸 Why "Avoid The Launch Window" Isn't The Only Strategy

Everything 56ViceLane has covered about GTA6's release window so far has followed the same pattern: studios moving their own launches away from it. No Rest For The Wicked got pushed to March 2027. Squadron 42 delayed specifically to dodge what its founder called "the attention buzz saw of GTA6." Even Ananta landed its release date two full months out. Shrek 2 Remastered is the first thing we've covered that went the other way — leaning straight into the hype instead of running from it.

📝 Editor's Note

There's a version of industry-timing coverage that's all delays and anxious studios hoping nobody notices their game the same week as GTA6. This is the rare counter-example — a publisher correctly betting that "parody the biggest launch of the decade" generates more attention than it costs, for a remaster that was never going to compete on its own merits anyway. Genuinely well played.

The Bottom Line

Shrek 2 Remastered is out now, discounted, and openly riffing on GTA6's marketing instead of hiding from it — a different approach than every other release-window story we've covered this year, and arguably the smarter one for a game this size.
