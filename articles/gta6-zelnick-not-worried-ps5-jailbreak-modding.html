Take-Two's CEO Isn't Worried About GTA6 Players Jailbreaking PS5s To Mod It

📅 November 10, 2026 ✍️ Marcus Webb ⏱️ 4 min read

According to GamesRadar, Take-Two CEO Strauss Zelnick says he isn't worried about GTA6 players jailbreaking their PS5 consoles to mod the game — reasoning that if the game itself is great, the monetization takes care of itself.

💬 The Actual Business Logic Here

This is a genuinely interesting admission from the person who ultimately answers to GTA6's investors: Zelnick isn't saying modding/jailbreaking doesn't happen, or that Take-Two is powerless to stop it — he's saying it doesn't fundamentally threaten the business, because a great enough game sells itself and its ongoing content regardless of how many players are tinkering with console-level mods. It's a confidence statement about the strength of the product itself, not a statement about piracy enforcement specifically (that's a separate, more aggressive effort — see Take-Two's parallel crackdown on AI-generated pre-release "recomps").

🔧 Why Modding And Piracy Aren't The Same Conversation

It's worth drawing a clear line here: jailbreaking a console to run mods on a legitimately-owned copy of a game is a very different category of behavior than piracy or unauthorized pre-release recreation. GTA5's modding scene — on PC especially — has been a genuine part of the game's decade-plus longevity, not something that undermined it. Zelnick's comment reads as Take-Two acknowledging that same dynamic will likely continue with GTA6, and that it isn't automatically a problem worth losing sleep over.

🎮 What This Means For You

If you're a modder who's been nervous about how aggressively Take-Two might go after console-modding specifically, this comment reads as reassuring — the company's top executive is on record saying it's not a top concern for the business. That said, this isn't a green light for piracy or anything that crosses into actually stealing the game itself; the distinction matters.

📝 Editor's Note

There's something almost refreshingly honest about a CEO saying the quiet part out loud — "if the game is good, this stuff doesn't matter" is the kind of confidence you don't hear from every publisher. Whether that confidence is earned depends entirely on how GTA6 itself actually lands in ten days.

The Bottom Line

Zelnick's comment is a small moment, but it tells you something real about how Take-Two is thinking heading into launch: focused on the game being good enough to make the rest a non-issue, rather than locking everything down out of fear.
