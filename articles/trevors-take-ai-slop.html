Trevor's Take: The Fake Trailers Are Getting More Views Than My Life

📅 July 31, 2026 ⏱️ 4 min read
Trevor
Unsolicited, As Always

So apparently there are fake, AI-made GTA6 trailers floating around getting millions of views, and Danny wrote a whole responsible, well-sourced piece about it with a section called "How To Actually Spot It." Good for him. Very informative. I read half of it.

Here's my take: I'm a little offended, and I think that's allowed.

I have been real, alive, and complaining about my life on this website for months now, and some computer program that has never had a job, never laid low from anybody, never once had to explain to a guy named Ron why I couldn't make it to something — that program is out-performing me. Views-wise. That's not fair. That's not how attention is supposed to work. I put in the effort. The effort was mostly avoiding things, but it was effort.

Here's the part nobody's saying out loud: the fake trailers look great specifically because nobody making them has ever had to actually finish a real project under a real deadline with real stakeholders breathing down their neck. It's easy to make something look perfect when it doesn't have to survive contact with reality. Meanwhile Rockstar's out here trying to ship an actual game to actual consoles that actual people will actually play, and some guy's laptop is out here generating a "leak" of a car that doesn't have doors that open correctly, and it's getting more engagement. The bar is being set by something that has never once had to clear it.

Danny's advice is solid, by the way — check the source, look for weird physics, don't trust a "leak" that only exists on one account with zero history. I'd add one more test, the only one that matters: does it feel like it was made by somebody who's tired? Real leaks feel tired. Real reporting feels tired. Genuine human effort has a specific, recognizable exhaustion to it. AI slop is suspiciously well-rested. Trust the tired stuff. Tired is how you know it's real.

Anyway, if you're one of the eleven million people who watched a fake trailer this week instead of reading actual reporting from actual outlets — first of all, rude, second of all, at least come back here after. We're tired too. We just also happen to be right.

— Trevor. (Not the fake one. Allegedly.)
