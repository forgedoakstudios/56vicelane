GTA6 Is Already Being Officially Censored In China

📅 September 3, 2026 ✍️ Marcus Webb ⏱️ 4 min read

GTA6 hasn't even launched and it's already getting the censor's scissors. The Extended Look trailer is live on Bilibili — China's biggest video platform — for local fans, but it isn't the same cut the rest of the world got. Some of it is missing, and the reasons why say a lot about what launching in the world's largest gaming market actually costs.

🎬 What's Different About The China Cut

According to The Gamer, the version of the Extended Look uploaded to Bilibili trims content that would typically run afoul of China's content regulators — material touching on violence, drug use, and other themes Chinese censors routinely flag before a foreign release is allowed to circulate. This is a well-worn playbook for AAA games and films entering the Chinese market, not something unique to Rockstar, but it's the first confirmation that GTA6 itself is already being run through that process.

🌏 Why This Happens At All

China's National Radio and Television Administration and its gaming-content regulators have long required edits to violent or drug-referencing content before foreign media can be legally distributed or streamed there. Grand Theft Auto has never been an easy fit for that filter — the series' entire identity is built on the exact content categories Chinese regulators scrutinize hardest. Getting any official GTA6 footage onto a platform like Bilibili at all is notable; getting it there without heavy edits was never realistic.

🎮 What This Means For You

If you're not playing on a China-region storefront, this changes nothing about your copy of the game. What it does confirm is that Take-Two and Rockstar are actively working through China's approval process ahead of launch — a market this large doesn't get skipped by accident, and the fact that marketing material is already being cleared suggests a genuine push to have GTA6 available there, not just in the West.

📊 The Stakes For Rockstar

China is the largest gaming market on the planet by revenue and player count, and previous GTA titles have had a complicated, often informal relationship with the country — official distribution has historically lagged behind grey-market and pirated access. A cleared, censored Extended Look on a platform as mainstream as Bilibili is a small but real signal that Take-Two wants an actual, sanctioned presence in China this time around, not just fans finding their own way in.

📝 Editor's Note

Worth separating two things here: censorship for a Chinese release is standard practice, not a Rockstar-specific controversy — Cyberpunk 2077, Elden Ring, and plenty of other violent AAA titles went through the same process. The actual story is the market signal — Rockstar clearing hurdles this early means China access is a real priority, not an afterthought bolted on after launch.

The Bottom Line

A trimmed trailer on Bilibili isn't a huge event on its own, but it's the clearest sign yet that GTA6's international rollout includes a genuine push into China — historically the toughest market for this franchise to officially crack. Expect more region-specific edits and approval milestones as launch gets closer.

As first reported by The Gamer.
