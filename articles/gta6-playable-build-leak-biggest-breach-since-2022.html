GTA6 Leak Escalates: Hacker Now Confirmed To Have A Full Playable Build

📅 August 20, 2026 ✍️ Ezra Voss ⏱️ 4 min read

The GTA6 leak we covered two days ago just got a lot more serious. New footage from the leaker calling themselves Cyberleek shows something specific enough to settle a real question: is this person just re-sharing old footage, or do they actually have the game itself? Multiple outlets now say it's the latter — a genuine playable build, not just video clips.

🕹️ The Proof That Changed The Story

According to The Gamer, corroborated by Rock Paper Shotgun and GamesRadar, the latest clip ends with protagonist Jason shooting a wall to spell out the word "Leek." That's not something you get from re-uploading someone else's old video — it requires actually controlling the character in a live build. Several other independent outlets (TechSpot, Push Square, 9to5Toys) landed on the same conclusion within hours of each other, which is a meaningfully stronger signal than any single outlet's claim alone.

📊 How Big Is This, Actually?

Multiple outlets are describing this as the biggest breach against Rockstar and Take-Two since the 2022 hack, when over 90 videos and screenshots leaked at once. This is a slower, more deliberate drip — Cyberleek is reportedly releasing new footage roughly every other day rather than dumping everything at once — but the underlying access appears comparable in scale. That's a meaningful distinction from the 8/18 leak we first covered, where the confirmed material was clips and a map image, not evidence of an actual working build.

🎯 What This Means For You

Nothing about your November 19 release date changes here — this doesn't affect the game you'll actually get. What it does mean: expect the drip of leaked footage to keep coming for a while, and expect Rockstar's own official marketing (like the August 27 Netflix Extended Look) to land in a noisier environment than the studio planned for.

🗣️ The Leaker's Stated Demands

Per the same reporting, Cyberleek claims to be motivated by opposition to digital-only distribution and pre-order-heavy launches, framing the leaks as protest rather than pure theft-for-profit — though (as we noted in our first report on this story) that framing should be treated as the leaker's own claim, not verified fact. Advocacy group Stop Killing Games has publicly distanced itself from the leaker despite sharing some of the same stated concerns about digital preservation, explicitly condemning the use of illegal means.

📝 Editor's Note

We're keeping this at "reported," not "confirmed" — Rockstar and Take-Two haven't issued an on-record statement acknowledging the scope of what's been accessed, and we can't independently verify the leaked footage ourselves. But the convergence across 8+ separate trusted outlets, all landing on "this is a real, unusually serious breach" within the same news cycle, is about as strong a signal as this kind of story gets short of an official statement.

The Bottom Line

What started as leaked clips and a map image two days ago has escalated into something closer to a real security incident — a leaker with apparent hands-on access to a working build, releasing footage on a drip schedule with no clear end date. Worth watching closely heading into Rockstar's own official reveal on the 27th.

Reported as first covered by The Gamer, Rock Paper Shotgun, and GamesRadar.
