Another GTA6 Map Tease Just Turned Up Inside GTA5

A painting found inside GTA Online, flipped upside down, looks a lot like the fan-built GTA6 map project. It's happened before. It's probably going to happen again. Here's what's actually new about this one.

56ViceLane Staff  •  Leak Watch · GTA6

Reddit user JaneBunnFan found it first: inside a building next to a tall plant in GTA Online, there's a painting nobody had paid much attention to — a large, bold green blob in the middle, surrounded by smaller, darker sections. Ordinary set dressing, easy to walk right past. Except when you flip the image 180 degrees, it starts looking a lot like the outline fans have been building for years as their best guess at GTA6's map.

Cue the usual reaction: screenshots everywhere, side-by-side comparisons, a fresh wave of "is this really it" posts. We'll give you the honest version instead of the hype version.

This Isn't The First Time

It genuinely isn't. A similar image turned up recently tied to The Kortz Center Heist update, and this general category of "in-game art secretly teases the next map" theory has been a recurring thread in the community for a long time now. Rockstar has a real history of hiding things in plain sight across its games — that part isn't in dispute. What's harder to prove is whether any specific painting is an intentional teaser versus a coincidence fans are pattern-matching onto, especially the second or third time a very similar claim shows up.

None of this is confirmed by Rockstar. It never is, by design — that's the whole game they play with easter eggs, real or imagined. Treat this one the same way as the last one: interesting, fun to look at, not something to build expectations around.

Worth Watching Instead

The more interesting detail buried in this story isn't the painting — it's the timing. Insider NateTheHate has said multiple times that August 2026 is the month for real official GTA6 footage, and floated the possibility it could be a full gameplay look rather than another trailer. If that's accurate, we won't need to squint at upside-down paintings much longer to know what the map actually looks like.
