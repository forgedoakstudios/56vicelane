Take-Two Says It's Not Interested In Selling Itself After EA's $55 Billion Buyout

📅 November 14, 2026 ✍️ Marcus Webb ⏱️ 5 min read

Five days before GTA6 launches, GameSpot is pointing out something that's easy to lose in the countdown noise: GTA6's own publisher just watched its biggest rival go private in one of the largest buyouts in gaming history — and has made clear it isn't interested in following suit.

💰 What Actually Happened With EA

Electronic Arts completed a roughly $55 billion take-private deal in August, led by Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund alongside Silver Lake and Affinity Partners — one of the largest leveraged buyouts in corporate history, taking EA off the public markets entirely. That's the real, confirmed event anchoring this story. According to GameSpot, Take-Two's own position on doing anything similar hasn't moved.

🏢 Zelnick's Actual Track Record On This

This isn't a one-off reaction. Take-Two CEO Strauss Zelnick has said on more than one occasion, across more than one context, that Take-Two intends to stay public and stay independent. In an August CNBC interview, he argued Take-Two can stand on its own — pointing to more than $8 billion in expected annual revenue and the company's track record of generating profit year after year, while being upfront that doing so "is not easy or predictable." On Take-Two's own earnings call reacting to EA's deal, he struck a notably unsentimental tone: no longer having a major public-company competitor "may give us a bit of room from a competitive point of view," he said, "but we never take anything for granted" — adding that a healthy industry is better served by multiple strong public players, not just one dominant private one.

🎮 Why This Matters Going Into Launch

It's a reminder that GTA6's publisher isn't just riding a wave of hype five days out — it's a company that's weathered real corporate pressure (EA reportedly tried to buy Take-Two outright back in 2008, right before GTA IV launched, and was turned down) and is choosing, deliberately and repeatedly, to stay a standalone public company through this launch rather than cash out. For a game this closely tied to one publisher's fortunes, that's not nothing.

📝 Editor's Note

Worth flagging plainly: this story connects a real, confirmed event (EA's $55B go-private deal) with real, previously-reported Zelnick quotes from separate occasions (the CNBC interview, the earnings call) rather than one single new on-the-record statement made this week. We couldn't independently pull GameSpot's own page directly from here to confirm their exact new framing, so we're treating this as a reported synthesis of well-established facts rather than a brand-new quote — the underlying claims themselves check out across multiple outlets.

The Bottom Line

Nothing here changes GTA6's November 19 launch. But as the industry's biggest publisher outside Take-Two just exited the public markets for $55 billion, it's a useful data point: the company behind the biggest game launch in years has had plenty of chances to cash out too, and keeps saying no.
