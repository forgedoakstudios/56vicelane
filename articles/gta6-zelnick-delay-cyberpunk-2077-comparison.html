Take-Two CEO Suggests Rockstar Avoided a Cyberpunk 2077-Style Launch With GTA6's Delays

📅 November 5, 2026 ✍️ Marcus Webb ⏱️ 4 min read

Take-Two CEO Strauss Zelnick has a new answer for why GTA6 got delayed twice, and it's less "we needed more polish" and more "look what happened to the other guy." He didn't say the name out loud. He didn't have to.

💬 What Zelnick Said

According to GamesRadar, reporting on a Wall Street Journal interview tied to GTA6's run-up to launch, Zelnick said he wasn't going to "throw any competitors under the bus" — then immediately described a high-profile release that shipped and then "had to pull the title after shipping." He never named a game. He didn't need to. As GamesRadar and other outlets covering the same interview point out, that description lines up almost exactly with one recent AAA launch: CD Projekt Red's Cyberpunk 2077, which was pulled from the PlayStation Store entirely in December 2020 after a disastrous, bug-riddled debut on last-gen consoles.

Worth being precise here: this is outlets reading between the lines, not Zelnick confirming he meant Cyberpunk 2077 by name. He was careful not to. But the shape of the comparison — a shipped title that got pulled rather than patched quietly — doesn't really describe anything else in recent memory.

🎮 Why This Matters

GTA6 has now been delayed twice, moving from its original 2025 window all the way to November 19, 2026 — something Zelnick has defended before as the cost of getting a 13-years-in-the-making game right. This is the clearest version yet of the argument: the alternative to delaying wasn't "ship on time," it was risking a launch bad enough to get pulled.

🧭 The Bigger Pattern

This fits a pattern Zelnick has leaned on throughout GTA6's delays — framing extra time as risk management rather than a sign of trouble. He's previously told investors that "when our competitors go to market before something was ready, bad things happen," and has called the delays "a wise investment" rather than a setback. The Cyberpunk comparison is the sharpest version of that argument yet: not just "we wanted more polish," but "look what happens when you don't take the time."

📝 Editor's Note

CEOs love this move — make the point without naming the competitor, let reporters and fans do the naming for you. It's a safer way to say "we made the right call" than pointing a finger directly. Doesn't make the underlying argument wrong, though: Cyberpunk 2077's launch is still the industry's go-to cautionary tale for shipping before a game (and its last-gen ports especially) was ready.

The Bottom Line

Nothing here changes GTA6's November 19, 2026 release date. But it's a notably blunt new justification for the two delays that got it there — straight from the top of Take-Two, in an interview clearly meant to get ahead of launch-week scrutiny.
