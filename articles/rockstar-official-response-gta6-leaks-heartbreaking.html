Rockstar Calls GTA6 Leaks "Heartbreaking" In First Official Response

📅 August 26, 2026 ✍️ Ezra Voss ⏱️ 4 min read

Weeks of silence just ended. Rockstar Games has issued its first official, on-record response to the CyberLeek gameplay leaks — and it's not a legal statement or a corporate non-answer. It's an apology, mixed with a promise that the leak won't change what's coming.

🗣️ What Rockstar Actually Said

According to Polygon, corroborated by GamesRadar, Dexerto, Push Square, Pure Xbox, and Kotaku, Rockstar told fans directly: "having videos of Grand Theft Auto VI gameplay leak in this way has been heartbreaking for our team, and this is obviously not how we intended for you to see the game after all this time." The studio went on to apologize for how long it's taken to reach the point of showing an official look at the game, while a high-ranking Rockstar executive denounced both the leak and the leaker but insisted the incident wouldn't detract from what the team calls "the excellent work" behind the upcoming release.

⏱️ Why Now

The timing isn't a coincidence. Rockstar's statement lands just one day before the official GTA6 Extended Look premieres on Netflix (August 27, with a YouTube release six hours later) — already covered here. Getting ahead of the leaks with a human, apologetic tone right before the real reveal reads like an attempt to reset the narrative: acknowledge the mess the CyberLeek leaks caused, then let the actual polished reveal do the talking.

🕵️ Where This Fits In The Leak Saga

This is Rockstar's first direct public comment since the CyberLeek leaks escalated — footage and a leaked map image first surfaced, followed by DMCA takedown efforts and reports of Take-Two pursuing subpoenas against Microsoft and Discord for identifying information tied to the leaker. None of that legal activity is addressed in this statement — this is purely Rockstar speaking for itself about how the leaks made the team feel, not a legal update.

🎯 What This Means For You

This doesn't change GTA6's November 19 release date or anything about the Extended Look airing tomorrow. What it does confirm: Rockstar is aware of exactly how much of the leaked footage has circulated, and is choosing to respond with an apology rather than silence — a notable shift after weeks of the studio saying nothing publicly.

📝 Editor's Note

We're marking this "confirmed" — the quoted statement is attributed directly to Rockstar Games and reported consistently, word-for-word, across multiple trusted outlets (Polygon, GamesRadar, Dexerto, Push Square, Pure Xbox, Kotaku), not a leak or an anonymous source.

The Bottom Line

Rockstar finally said something — and what it said was more human than most fans expected. "Heartbreaking" isn't corporate boilerplate; it's an admission that the leaks genuinely hurt, right before the studio gets to show the game on its own terms.
