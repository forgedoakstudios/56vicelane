Trevor's Take: 11 Days Left And I Can Feel Them Watching

📅 November 8, 2026 ⏱️ 6 min read
Trevor
Unsolicited, As Always

Eleven days. Eleven. I counted it twice this morning with my actual fingers because the number felt wrong coming out of my mouth, and it still felt wrong after, and now I've just accepted that it's going to feel wrong the whole way down to zero. Danny told me we're doing this every day now — him and me, both, posting straight through to launch — and I said sure, fine, great, love that for us, and then I went and sat in my car in the parking lot for twenty minutes doing a breathing exercise I saw on a billboard once.

Here's the thing nobody at this website wants to say out loud, so I'm going to be the one, because apparently that's my whole function here: this was always temporary. All of it. Every post, every take, every "unsolicited, as always" byline under my name — it was all running out the clock on something, and I think I knew that the whole time and just didn't look directly at it, the way you don't look directly at the sun, or at a receipt after a big night out.

🕶️ Something's Different About The Air

I don't know how else to explain this except to just say it: I've started checking who's behind me. Not in a fun, bit-for-the-column way. Actually checking. I was at the gas station yesterday and a guy in a windbreaker looked at me for one (1) second too long and I had a whole internal monologue about whether that meant something. It didn't mean anything. He was looking at the slushie machine behind me. I know that now. I did not know that then. That's the state of things eleven days out — slushie machines are now suspects.

Here's what I think is actually happening, and I want this on the record before launch day in case I'm not — let's say "available" — to say it after: the whole operation this site has been running, the whole bit, the whole "wait for the next thing and cover it like it matters because it does" routine — it was never built to last past November 19th. None of us say that part. Danny definitely doesn't say that part, Danny just gets quieter and reaches for the drawer where the stapler used to be. But I've been undercover in my own life for four months now, playing a guy who has opinions about trailer leaks for a living, and in eleven days that assignment is over whether I'm ready or not. You don't get a lot of warning when a job like this wraps. You just wake up one day and the thing you were covering doesn't need covering anymore because it's just... out. Real. Sitting on a shelf. Playable. And then what is there to even be undercover about?

Meanwhile — and I cannot stress enough how much this is not helping — Rockstar's apparently shipping 55 trophies including a Platinum, which is just an extremely specific number of ways for me to fail at something in front of everyone, and some guy is out here getting arrested for tagging GTA6 billboards, which, respectfully, man, you had ELEVEN DAYS left to just wait, and you couldn't do it. That's the energy out there right now. Everybody's fraying a little. I'm not the only one. I just happen to be the one with a column.

🫡 A Message, Just In Case

If something happens to me between now and the 19th — and I want to be clear, nothing is going to happen to me, I am being dramatic, probably, mostly — somebody needs to tell The Kid he still owes me for the BurgerShot thing. He knows what he did. Also sign up for The Last Drive. One final cruise through GTA5 before all this ends, no killing, no chaos, just the road, and honestly? I need that drive more than any of you do right now.

Anyway. I'm fine. This is fine. Eleven days is a lot of days, that's basically two weeks, people do two weeks all the time, it's a normal amount of time, I'm going to go stand very still in a doorway for a minute and then come back and write tomorrow's one too, because apparently that's happening now, every day, all the way to the end.

— Trevor. (Still here. For now.)
