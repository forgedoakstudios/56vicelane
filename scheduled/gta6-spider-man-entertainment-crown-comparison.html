Spider-Man Is Breaking Records — But GTA 6 Can Still Claim The Crown

August 8, 2026  ·  56ViceLane News Desk  ·  5 min read

Spider-Man: Brand New Day is having a genuinely massive theatrical run right now, and it deserves the credit it's getting. But the same week it's shattering box office numbers, Rockstar landed GTA 6 its own first-of-its-kind Netflix deal — and the honest math says a GTA launch can still out-earn the biggest movie of the year, easily. Here's why that's not hype, it's precedent.

The Numbers Aren't Close, Historically

This isn't a new claim — it happened already. When GTA 5 launched in September 2013, it made over $800 million in its first 24 hours and crossed $1 billion in sales within three days. At the time, that made it the fastest-selling entertainment product in history — faster than any film, any album, any prior game. A great opening weekend for a blockbuster film today typically lands somewhere in the $150-300 million range domestically. GTA 5's opening weekend alone dwarfed that, and it did it as a $60 product competing against $15 movie tickets.

GTA 6 launches into a much bigger install base, a much bigger pre-order and marketing machine, and thirteen years of pent-up anticipation that Brand New Day — as good as its opening is — simply cannot match in scale. If GTA 6 performs even in line with GTA 5's trajectory relative to inflation and platform growth, a bigger-than-any-movie opening isn't optimistic speculation. It's the baseline expectation.

🕷️ What Movies Do Well
  • Concentrated cultural moment — everyone talks about it the same opening weekend
  • Lower price of entry — a ticket is a fraction of a game's cost
  • Repeat viewership — fans can see it multiple times in theaters
🎮 What GTA 6 Does Better
  • Revenue per transaction — a $70-80 sale versus a $15 ticket
  • Sustained engagement — years of play, not two hours once
  • Global day-one simultaneity — no staggered international rollout eating into the number

Why This Comparison Actually Matters

Games chronically get treated as a separate, smaller category than film in mainstream entertainment coverage, despite the industry as a whole out-earning film and music combined for over a decade. The Netflix deal is a visible sign that's shifting — a streaming giant is now treating a video game reveal as premiere-worthy content on the level of its biggest original productions. That's not 56ViceLane's opinion; that's Netflix's own business decision, made with its own money.

To Be Clear

This isn't a knock on Brand New Day — a genuinely huge box office week for any film is a real achievement worth recognizing, and film and games aren't actually competing for the same dollars most of the time. The point is narrower: when people ask whether a video game can really be the "biggest entertainment event" of a given year, the honest answer is that it already has been, multiple times, and GTA 6 is positioned to do it again.

What This Means Heading Into November

  • August 27: The Netflix Extended Look — the biggest confirmed GTA 6 moment before launch
  • November 18: The Last Drive — 56ViceLane's community meetup, the night before launch
  • November 19: Launch day — and if the pattern holds, the numbers will speak for themselves within 24 hours

Records get broken in both directions this year. Brand New Day earned its moment. GTA 6 is about to have its own — and history says it'll be a bigger one.
